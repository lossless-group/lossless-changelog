# Three directions for Threshold

## Why Care?

The site being replaced is a hosted-storefront template with a 2008 information
architecture — condensed all-caps headlines, one green button, a value-stack
offer page that ends in *"WAIT, THAT'S NOT ALL!!"*, and two blocks of unreplaced
Lorem ipsum sitting in production where the team bios belong.

The interesting problem underneath is not the styling. It is that the site
**introduces a coined product name and three clinical terms of art before it has
told a stranger what the thing is**, and that its single purchase decision has no
upgrade path at all. The client is also mid-transition from practitioner to
thought leader and is shy about it, so any answer that depends on her performing
is the wrong answer.

## What shipped

Three complete homepages at `/journal`, `/atlas` and `/practice`, plus a chooser
at `/`, an article corpus at `/articles`, and the two required reference pages.

All three run the **identical content collections** — twelve articles, eight
episodes, three bundle tiers — so the comparison is about form, not copy.

- **Journal** — the site as a clinical publication. Authority from the corpus and
  the bylines rather than from volume. Cart renders as a `ledger`.
- **Atlas** — an anatomical axis runs down the page and every section docks to a
  point on it, so the argument's geometry is visible before you read a word.
  Cart renders as a `compare` table.
- **Practice** — opens on the symptom in the reader's own words, in her voice, at
  conversational volume. Cart renders as `steps`.

## The three things worth keeping

**Orientation encoded in the schema, not in a style note.** The `articles`
collection carries `plain_question` — the jargon-free question someone actually
types at 11pm — and every index card renders it *above* the clinical title. A
`term_of_art` / `term_in_plain_english` pair rides along so a term never appears
without its translation within a few pixels. A rule that lives in the data model
survives a template rewrite; a rule that lives in a design doc does not.

**Supersets are what make one-click upgrade honest.** Each tier is a strict
superset of the one below, so upgrading never removes anything, so the cart can
present the *delta* — the two lines you are adding and the price difference —
rather than swapping in a whole new cart the reader has to re-read. The
collection stores the full include list per tier and the renderer diffs adjacent
tiers itself, so nobody maintains two lists by hand. A locked line item is a real
affordance: click the thing you want and you move to the cheapest tier that has
it.

**One illustration family, nine appearances.** Seven ink-on-paper plates
generated off one locked seed, deliberately desaturated. Rather than ship a light
set and a dark set, `Plate.astro` dissolves the ground with a blend mode —
`multiply` in light, `invert` + `screen` in dark and vibrant — and tints the ink
toward the active `--color-primary`. Three directions times three modes, no
duplicated assets.

## Flagged

The live site publishes exactly one price. Tier *contents* here are all real line
items from the client's own offer page; the **per-tier prices are invented** to
demonstrate the mechanic. They are not a pricing recommendation.

Article bodies render through Astro's native markdown pipeline rather than
`@lossless-group/lfm`. Acceptable for a spike, first thing to fix if it proceeds.
