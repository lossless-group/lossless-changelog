## Why Care?

The plan at `/psp` says what I'll do for 100 days. Nothing showed whether I'm
doing it, or let my coach and cohort see that at a glance.

## What's New?

- **`/psp/dashboard`**: where the 100 days stand. A goals × weeks grid
  (target hit, floor met, below floor, in progress, not logged yet), the
  Day 30, 60, and 90 checkpoints with habits against the paced plan and
  outcomes against their milestones, and this week so far. Written for a
  reader who hasn't seen the plan.
- **`/psp/dashboard/week/N`**: one program week in detail. Per goal, a 7-day
  grid of daily habits, weekly habits on the days they happened, and a week
  total against that week's floor and target.
- **`/psp/dashboard/sample`**: the same views with a fictional member forty
  days in, clearly bannered, so the dashboard can be understood before my
  own data fills it in.

## How it Works

The plan becomes numbers in `src/content/psp/tracking/mpstaton/tracker.yaml`:
each goal's habits with per-week floors and targets (the ramp and the
deload weeks are encoded as overrides), and outcomes with milestones.
What happened goes in one `log/week-NN.yaml` per program week, written by
whatever assistant runs the check-in, or by hand.

Two rules shape the views. A day nobody has logged yet is hatched, never
red, because catching up on Friday for the whole week is normal. And an
in-progress week only shows good news early: a target already hit says
so, and everything else reads "In progress" rather than "Below floor" on
a Thursday.

The rollup logic (`src/lib/psp/tracker.ts`) is plain TypeScript with no
framework imports. The routes render per request so the current week is
always right, and the YAML is bundled at build time with
`import.meta.glob`, so there's no runtime filesystem read on Vercel.

The format and rules are written up as general guidelines in hope-ai
(`references/dashboard-guidelines.md`), with this implementation copied
there as the reference.
