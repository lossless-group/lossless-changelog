# A Fourth Direction: Mandatory Ventures as a Broadsheet Front Page

## Why Care?

Three directions went up last week ([[2026-11-14_01]]): Label, Annual Report and Paste-Up. Then, while building redesign options for a different site, one design kept being the right one for *this* site: an editorial front page that makes a single argument in big type. So we lifted it out and set it on Mandatory's own copy, so it can be judged like-for-like against the other three.

Open `/broadsheet/` next to `/label/`, `/report/` and `/paste-up/`. Same words, four ways to print them.

## What's New?

- **`/broadsheet/`**: the home page as a newspaper front page. Nameplate masthead, "Coordinating capital into *the world's most important companies.*" set at poster size, the subhead as a drop-cap deck, the definition of *man•da•to•ry* as a dictionary sidebar.
- **Numbered sections** (No. 01 to No. 05) in the narrative's order:
  - The startup-to-scaleup curve is redrawn as "Fig. 1", with its three annotations in ruled columns and "This is when everyone starts pretending" circled in red pen.
  - The two voices are pull quotes, large-scale capital first and set larger.
  - People and trust is a masthead roster.
  - "Why this one" is an op-ed spread.
  - "No entity. No admin." becomes two big figures.
- **Closing line:** "Because it's *mandatory.*", as large as the headline.
- **`/broadsheet/reading/because-its-mandatory/`**: Mandatory Reading in the same editorial style.
- **Modes:** light (black on bone paper, pen blue as the one accent), dark (cream on carbon) and vibrant (navy, with the front page and closing printed as solid blue panels).

## How It Maps to the Brand Guidance

Broadsheet keeps the spine and the inks, and departs from the mood board in four deliberate places. These are the questions to answer before it's chosen:

| Mood board says | Broadsheet does |
|---|---|
| Three type voices: lowercase italic serif, spaced geometric caps, typewriter | Fraunces (display), Newsreader (reading), IBM Plex Mono (labels) |
| One red hairline rule | Heavy ink rules, thick over thin; red stays the editor's pen only |
| Print texture: grain, misregistration, felt-tip | Light grain and a slightly rough chart line; no misregistration |
| Silhouette illustration, Nitsche bands | All type, no illustration |

Red is still scarce: editor's notes, the circle on Fig. 1's third point, and the focus ring.

## Under the Hood

- **Same contract as the other three.** One theme file (`src/styles/themes/broadsheet.css`), its own components (`src/components/broadsheet/`), its own home and reading pages, no imports from another direction, no hex values or Tier 1 tokens in components.
- **Word budget holds.** Built-HTML counts: label 410, report 483, paste-up 450, broadsheet 465, all under 520. The only added words are furniture: section numbers, "Fig. 1", "Masthead", "Colophon", "Connect".
- **Adding a fourth direction touched shared files**, because a few places assumed exactly three:
  - The chooser had a hard-coded list and "three ways" in its heading. It now has a fourth card and a dynamic count.
  - `BoilerPlateHTML.astro`'s `Direction` type and `theme-switcher.js`'s `VALID_THEMES` both gained `broadsheet`.
  - The design-system page builds a component list per entry in `DIRECTIONS`, so it needed one for broadsheet.
  - `global.css` gained three Tier 1 font tokens. Newsprint reuses the existing bone paper.
- **Known gap.** Broadsheet loads its fonts from inside the page body, so the design-system page shows its preview in fallback serifs.

## What's Next

- Decide whether Broadsheet's type system replaces the mood board's three voices, or whether it should be re-set in them.
- If Broadsheet wins: retire the other three directions (delete their folders and routes) and promote it to `/`.
- Load Broadsheet's fonts in the shared head so the design-system preview matches.
