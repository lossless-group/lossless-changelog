## Why Care?

A retrieval corpus is only as good as the metadata it is indexed on. The
changelog tier was swept in a prior pass; this one finishes the job across
`context-v/`, the larger and messier half — full of stubs, drifted dates, and
documents that had never had frontmatter at all. The corpus that feeds Chroma
is deliberately wider than the swept tier and now stands at 1,166 files. With that settled, the corpus was re-indexed and, for the
first time, pushed to Chroma Cloud so retrieval is not bound to one laptop.

## What's New?

**The sweep.** Every `context-v/` document across the tree was brought to
frontmatter conformity: identity (`site_uuid`, `hex_code`) minted where
missing and never overwritten where present, editorial dates reconciled
against filesystem and git evidence, ledes and summaries authored, and a
publish decision recorded per document. Thirty-eight files that had no
frontmatter block at all got one. Eighteen unparseable YAML blocks were
repaired. A confidentiality screen ran over the public repos, genericizing
client and portfolio names where naming them added nothing.

**Accuracy, not just conformity.** The date work was the substantive part.
Thirteen date-resolution chains predated the editorial-date convention and
silently resolved to nothing, leaving sixteen documents rendering blank dates
on the sites. Client-engagement documents kept their original dates rather
than being re-stamped, because the dates are evidence of when the work
happened.

**Robustness.** Nine hand-rolled frontmatter parsers across the splash sites
mishandled YAML block scalars (`>-`, `|-`), turning a folded lede into the
literal string `>-` — one site shipped that to production in a meta tag. All
nine were patched and proven functionally identical, which makes the
consolidation onto LFM provably safe. Logged as an issue in `astro-knots`.

**Chroma.** The corpus was re-collated (it had drifted a month) and
re-ingested: **10,179 chunks** in `context-vigilance-corpus`, 522 in
`lossless-changelog`.

**Chroma Cloud.** All 10,179 chunks now live in the `lossless-corpus` cloud
database. Getting there required two fixes to `ingest-to-chroma.py`, both of
which had been latent:

- `chunk_by_heading` split on `## ` and nothing else, so a document with no
  `##` headings became a single chunk — the largest was 73KB against Cloud's
  16KB per-document limit. It now sub-splits on `### `, then paragraph breaks,
  then a UTF-8-safe hard cut.
- Chunk IDs embed the full source path and two exceeded the 128-byte ID limit.
  Overflow IDs now carry a path hash; the other 10,177 keep their readable
  form, because a readable ID is what makes a raw collection dump diagnosable.

**Graphiti.** The changelog knowledge graph is ingesting into Neo4j — 522
dated entries as bi-temporal episodes. It runs at roughly one entry every
25 seconds and persists state after each success, so it resumes from wherever
it stops.

## What's next

- Finish the Graphiti pass and query the graph for what the changelog tier
  can answer that vector search cannot.
- The sweep surfaced 22 documents whose sections blew the size cap. They are
  the corpus telling us which documents have outgrown themselves, and are the
  natural candidates for the fork-and-cross-reference discipline.
- Two artifacts in the corpus are not documents at all — a generated wikilink
  path audit and a hash-named scrape cache. They probably should not be
  indexed.
